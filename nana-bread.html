Nana Bread

A Perth bakery that makes gourmet banana bread to order, sells at the markets, and had outgrown taking orders by direct message.

Visit nanabreadperth.com ↗

Year
2025
Sector
Food & beverage, bake to order
Scope
Brand application, storefront, checkout, pickup logic, wholesale enquiries
Tom / Andy
Structure, copy, storefront design / ecommerce build, payments, fulfilment logic
01 — The problem

Demand was never the problem.

Orders were arriving as direct messages and being reconciled by hand. That works right up until it doesn’t: a business run that way is capped at the number of messages one person can answer in an evening, and that person is the same one who has to be up baking.

It also produces the worst possible failure: taking money for something that was never going to exist, because the tray was already spoken for.

A small business, said plainly

The range, priced and ready to order

02 — What we built

A storefront that knows the baking schedule.

The range, the prices and the pickup windows live in one place, and the checkout knows all three. You choose your treats and a pickup slot in the same step, which is the order people think in, not a cart, then a surprise about collection.

Wholesale and catering sit on their own path, because a café ordering weekly needs a different conversation to someone buying a loaf for Saturday.

The arrangement, stated before the checkout

03 — The mechanism

The cut-off follows the baking schedule, not the clock.

Orders close on Thursday evening for the coming pickup window, and the production limits sit inside the checkout rather than in someone’s head. When the day’s tray is gone, the shop closes itself.

Bake-to-order is unfamiliar to most people buying online. They expect a box tomorrow. So the arrangement is stated on the page before anyone reaches the checkout, which removes almost every question the business used to answer by message.

04 — Where it is ordered

On a phone, usually on a Thursday.

Close to all of this happens on a phone, often straight from a post. Ordering is one tap from the first screen, and the market banner does the job the caption used to do: telling a regular where to find them this weekend.
