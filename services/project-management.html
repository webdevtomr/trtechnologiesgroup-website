Project & programme delivery

Make ownership, decisions and dependencies clear across the people delivering a change.

Discuss your project

Where this helps.

Several teams and vendors are involved, but there is no shared view of the work. Milestones move without a clear decision. Reporting is extensive while the real blockers remain unresolved.

Project management should make delivery easier to steer. The useful questions are what is blocked, who can resolve it and what decision is needed next.

What the work involves.

Establish the delivery picture

Review objectives, scope, commitments, dependencies and available capacity. Make differences in expectations visible before turning them into a plan.

Set practical governance

Agree decision rights, escalation routes, reporting and a working cadence. Keep responsibility for delivery distinct from authority to approve changes.

Coordinate the work

Maintain a shared view of milestones, risks, issues and actions. Bring business owners and delivery teams together around decisions that affect the outcome.

Plan transition early

Include acceptance, operational readiness, support and handover in the delivery plan. Close the project against agreed responsibilities, not simply a launch date.

What you leave with.

  • Integrated delivery plan and decision cadence
  • Risk, issue, dependency and action registers
  • Stakeholder reporting focused on decisions
  • Acceptance and transition plan

Deliverables and acceptance criteria are agreed at the start. They are working materials for your team, with the level of detail matched to the decisions and implementation ahead.

Starting together.

Existing plans, commitments and reporting, plus access to the sponsor and workstream owners. An imperfect current picture is a valid starting point.

An engagement can coordinate delivery without taking over your vendors’ technical accountability. Roles and authority are made explicit in the scope.
