Workflow automation & integration

Connect the handoffs between people and systems so routine work can move without constant copying, chasing and checking.

Discuss your project

Where this helps.

The same information is entered in several places. Work waits in inboxes. A process runs smoothly until an exception appears, then nobody knows who owns it.

Automating the normal path is only part of the job. The design also needs to explain what happens when information is missing, an integration fails or a person needs to make a decision.

What the work involves.

Map one complete transaction

Follow an item from arrival to completion. Record the information, systems, approvals and manual checks at each step, including the informal workarounds.

Design the rules and exceptions

Specify what can proceed automatically and what needs review. Agree who receives exceptions, what context they need and how the work resumes.

Build a bounded flow

Connect the necessary systems using agreed access. Test normal cases, duplicates, missing information and interrupted processing before opening the workflow to wider use.

Make operation visible

Agree useful logs, alerts and an owner for changes. Provide a manual recovery path and a clear handover so the process is maintainable after launch.

What you leave with.

  • Current and future workflow maps
  • Integration scope and field mapping
  • Working automation with exception paths
  • Operating notes, monitoring and handover

Deliverables and acceptance criteria are agreed at the start. They are working materials for your team, with the level of detail matched to the decisions and implementation ahead.

Starting together.

The people who run the workflow and the owners of the systems involved. A few difficult examples are as useful as the normal ones.

Access to third-party systems, platform licences and vendor restrictions affect the scope. These are checked before committing to an implementation.
