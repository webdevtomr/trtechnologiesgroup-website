Project review & recovery planning

Get a clear view of a project that has lost momentum, then decide what to reset, resolve or stop.

Discuss your project

Where this helps.

Confidence in the plan is falling. Different people describe different problems. More reporting has not clarified whether the project is achievable, properly owned or still solving the right problem.

A review is useful when it leads to decisions. The aim is to separate evidence from assumptions and make the options visible, without promising that every troubled project can be recovered.

What the work involves.

Reconstruct the current position

Review the intended outcome, commitments, scope changes and delivery evidence. Speak with the sponsor, business owner and the people doing the work.

Test the critical assumptions

Examine dependencies, capacity, unresolved decisions and the basis for current estimates. Identify where the plan relies on something that has not yet been demonstrated.

Set out realistic options

Describe what continuing, re-sequencing, reducing scope or pausing would mean. Make the consequences and unknowns visible for the decision-maker.

Agree the reset

If the project proceeds, establish immediate actions, owners and review points. Confirm how progress and confidence will be assessed after the reset.

What you leave with.

  • A concise assessment of the current position
  • Critical assumptions, risks and unresolved decisions
  • Options and their implications
  • An agreed reset plan or recommendation to pause

Deliverables and acceptance criteria are agreed at the start. They are working materials for your team, with the level of detail matched to the decisions and implementation ahead.

Starting together.

The current plan, key commitments, recent reporting and candid access to the people involved.

We agree the review scope, access to evidence and any need for independent specialist advice before starting.
