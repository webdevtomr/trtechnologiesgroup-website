01 — The problem

Expertise with a calendar for a ceiling.

The knowledge was real and the demand was proven, but it could only be delivered one way: by one person, in a room, at consulting rates. That model has a hard limit, and the limit is a diary.

The businesses who needed it most were the ones least able to afford it: small operators who still have to produce a SWMS before they can get on site, keep contractor insurances current, and have something defensible if an incident happens.

Six separate obligations, usually held in six separate places

02 — What we built

RMSuite. One login, the whole obligation.

SWMS, incidents, employees, contractors and a library of more than 500 editable documents, in a single system rather than five. Alongside it, the commerce path for the parts that genuinely are off-the-shelf, because not every business needs the platform, and some just need the document.

03 — The decision that shaped it

Most of the work was leaving things out.

Every feature that would have needed a consultant to explain it was cut. A safety tool that requires a training session before anyone can use it is just consulting with extra steps. It would have rebuilt the exact ceiling the product existed to remove.

The same logic set the pricing. One subscription covers the whole platform and all 500-plus documents, priced only by the size of the team, so nobody has to work out which module they need before they have used it.

04 — In the field

Used on a site, on a phone, on one bar of signal.

Compliance software is not used at a desk. It is used standing on a job, by someone who has three minutes and gloves on. Everything that matters is reachable in one thumb-length, and nothing important depends on a connection that may not be there.
