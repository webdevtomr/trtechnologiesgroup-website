What happens
after launch?

Agree ownership, support and what your team needs from handover.

Read the guideWorking checklistDiscuss your project

A project can reach its launch date while the receiving team is still unprepared. Use the handover conversation to establish who will own the result, how it will be operated and what remains unresolved.

Agree who accepts the work.

Name the business owner and the operational owner. Make clear who can confirm that the deliverable meets its agreed scope and who will take responsibility for day-to-day use.

Check the real journey.

Walk through important tasks, exceptions and support situations with the receiving team. Capture unresolved issues and distinguish launch blockers from work that can follow.

Transfer access and knowledge.

Confirm that the right people can access the systems, documentation and supplier information they need. The organisation should know where its critical accounts and assets are controlled.

Rehearse a problem.

Discuss what happens when an integration stops, a user cannot complete a task or an owner is unavailable. Agree the support route and escalation responsibility.

Make the backlog explicit.

Record outstanding items, accepted limitations, workarounds and the owner of each next decision. A quiet omission is not an agreed handover.

Review after launch.

Set a review point to examine user feedback, support demand and whether the intended change is happening. Assign the person who can act on the findings.
