Before you fund
an AI pilot.

Define the problem, the data and what success would look like.

Read the guideWorking checklistDiscuss your project

The first pilot needs to answer a business question. Start by writing down the decision you want to make at the end: proceed, revise the approach or stop. A demonstration alone is not that decision.

Name one workflow.

Describe the trigger, the people involved, the information used and the point at which the work is complete. Avoid a brief such as “use AI in operations”; pick a task that can be observed.

Record the current position.

Use the measures relevant to the work: handling time, repeated checks, rework, response quality or effort. Record what is unknown rather than filling the gaps with assumed savings.

Write the boundaries.

Identify what the system may read, suggest or change. Specify what requires human approval, which cases are excluded and how a person can complete the work if the pilot fails.

Prepare representative examples.

Include ordinary cases and difficult ones. Agree who can assess the output and what makes it acceptable. Use information you are authorised to use for the test.

Count the whole workflow.

Include preparation, review, correction and support in the comparison. A quick generated output may still create substantial checking work elsewhere.

Set the decision meeting.

Name the owner, agree the evidence to review and define the next decision. Expanding the pilot should be a considered choice, not the default because a demo worked.
