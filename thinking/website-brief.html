A better website starts
with a better brief.

Clarify your audience, content and business requirements.

Read the guideWorking checklistDiscuss your project

A redesign brief should connect the business ambition with the decisions visitors need to make. Visual references matter, but they cannot decide what the site needs to explain or prove.

Define the primary visitors.

List the people the website must help and the situations that bring them there. A prospective buyer, a referrer and a job candidate may need different information and routes.

Name the decisions.

Write down what each visitor needs to understand before they act. Turn those questions into content requirements, rather than starting with a list of fashionable page sections.

Gather usable evidence.

Collect the work, people, outcomes and information you can show accurately. Identify permission requirements and proof gaps early. Do not substitute invented metrics or testimonials.

Map the paths.

Connect the homepage, capability details, relevant work and contact. Check whether a visitor entering on an internal page can still find the context they need.

Set the experience direction.

Choose references for specific reasons: typography, image treatment, navigation or motion. Explain what should be borrowed and what would feel wrong for the business.

Plan for ownership.

Decide who can update the site, which content changes regularly and how enquiries will be handled. Make maintenance and measurement part of the scope.
